Prior Authorization & Referrals

Verify requirements, secure approvals, track visits and resolve authorization denials.

What is the difference?

Prior authorization (PA)

A payer's advance review of a proposed service against coverage and medical-necessity requirements. An approval is not a guarantee of payment.

Referral

A direction or order from a PCP or other authorized clinician to a specialist or service. Some plans require a recorded referral; it is separate from PA.

Precertification / notification

Payer-specific terminology for advance review or notice. Determine whether it requires a decision or only notification.

Retrospective authorization

A post-service request allowed only in defined circumstances. Never assume it is available.

End-to-end workflow

  1. Verify active eligibility for the planned DOS, benefit coverage, network status, PCP and plan type.
  2. Check the payer's current authorization list using CPT/HCPCS, service location, provider and diagnosis. Determine referral requirements separately.
  3. Collect order, clinical notes, diagnosis, planned codes, units/visits, facility, rendering NPI and requested date range.
  4. Submit through designated portal, delegated utilization manager, phone or other permitted secure channel. Save confirmation and reference.
  5. Track pending requests and respond to missing information. Escalate urgent requests only when clinically appropriate.
  6. Review approval scope: exact code(s), laterality if relevant, provider, location, visits/units and validity dates. Record it in the scheduling and billing system.
  7. Recheck eligibility and available visits before treatment; request extension or additional units before exceeding approval.
  8. Link the authorization to the correct claim as required. Monitor adjudication and investigate mismatches.

Emergency servicesDo not delay emergency care to obtain PA; follow applicable notification rules.
Physical therapyCheck visit limits, authorized units versus visits, discipline, plan of care, start/end dates and extensions.

2026 CMS decision timeframes

CMS-0057-F requires certain impacted payers to decide medical-service PA requests within 7 calendar days (standard) or 72 hours (expedited), starting in 2026. Federally facilitated exchange QHP issuers are excluded from this particular timing requirement; drug authorizations are outside this rule. Check shorter applicable requirements and payer-specific procedures.
