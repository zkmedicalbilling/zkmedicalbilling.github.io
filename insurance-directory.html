INN vs OON provider billing

INN: Participating provider with a network agreement. Follow contracted filing deadlines, allowed amounts, adjustment requirements and dispute procedures.

OON: Nonparticipating provider. Verify benefits, authorization, noncontracted filing deadlines, appeal rights and applicable balance-billing protections. OON does not automatically mean patient responsibility.

For Medicare FFS, participating/nonparticipating status is not equivalent to commercial INN/OON. For Medicaid, enrollment and managed-care network participation must be checked separately.

NY-regulated commercial insurance and HMOs

Initial filing: generally 120 days from DOS, or a more favorable agreed period. Medicaid managed care: generally 90 days from DOS. Do not assume these rules govern self-funded ERISA, Medicare Advantage or other federally regulated products. INN providers may seek reconsideration of a solely late-filed claim in qualifying unusual circumstances; reimbursement may be reduced by up to 25%. OON: confirm statutory applicability and specific plan dispute rights; do not assume an identical contractual deadline.

NY Department of Financial Services — provider rights and prompt payment

Original Medicare — NY Part B

Initial filing: generally one calendar year from DOS. First-level redetermination: 120 days from receipt of determination. Minor corrections use reopening rather than appeal. Participating and nonparticipating Medicare providers are not commercial INN/OON categories.
