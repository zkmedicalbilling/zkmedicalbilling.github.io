In-Network (INN) & Out-of-Network (OON) Providers

Understand provider network participation, contracted rates, patient cost sharing, claim routing, and common revenue-cycle scenarios.

Important

Network status is specific to the exact provider, location, tax ID/NPI, product/network, and date of service. A provider may be INN for one plan and OON for another. Verify using the payer's provider directory and written eligibility/benefit information; do not rely only on the insurance card or a general brand name.

INN

In-Network Provider

A provider or facility that has a participation agreement for the member's specific network/product and is subject to that agreement's terms.

  • Contracted fee schedule or negotiated payment terms may apply.
  • Member generally receives the plan's in-network cost-sharing level.
  • Provider must follow applicable contract terms, authorization rules, and claim-filing requirements.
  • Contract may limit billing the member beyond permitted cost sharing.
OON

Out-of-Network Provider

A provider without a participating agreement for the member's specific network/product, subject to the plan's out-of-network benefit and applicable law.

  • Coverage may be lower, unavailable, or subject to a separate deductible.
  • Member cost sharing can be higher.
  • Plan may calculate payment using an allowed amount or other plan-defined method.
  • Balance billing may be possible where legally permitted; check federal/state protections and plan terms.

Key terms for billing teams

Contracted rateNegotiated INN payment terms

The agreement's rate or methodology for covered services. Confirm provider, location, code, modifier, and effective date.

Allowed amountPlan-recognized amount

The amount the plan recognizes for a covered service under its benefit rules. It may not equal the billed charge or provider's contracted rate.

Patient responsibilityDeductible, copay, coinsurance

Apply only the amount supported by the EOB/ERA and plan rules. Do not assume the full difference between charge and payment is patient responsibility.

Network verificationVerify exact product and service location

Confirm network, rendering/billing provider, facility, specialty, effective date, referral, authorization, and benefit limitations.

Common claim scenarios

Scenario 1 — INN provider, INN benefit

Check the contract rate and EOB/ERA adjustments. Post contractual adjustments according to the agreement; bill the member only valid cost sharing and other permitted amounts.

Scenario 2 — OON provider, PPO benefit

Confirm that OON benefits exist, the separate deductible/coinsurance, allowed-amount calculation, authorization, and whether the provider may bill beyond plan payment under applicable law and contract.

Scenario 3 — OON provider, HMO/EPO product

Do not assume routine OON services are covered. Check emergency exceptions, authorized exceptions, continuity-of-care rules, and the exact Evidence of Coverage before determining claim or patient liability.

Scenario 4 — Directory says INN but claim processes OON

Validate the exact provider NPI, TIN, service address, specialty, product/network, and DOS. Save directory evidence and payer reference number; request reprocessing or a network-status review when appropriate.

Federal patient-protection reminder

The No Surprises Act protects patients in certain situations, including many emergency services and certain out-of-network services at in-network facilities. It does not make every OON service in-network or eliminate all balance billing. Check the exact service, provider, facility, notice/consent rules, and applicable state law.
