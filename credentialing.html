Provider Credentialing & Enrollment

A practical step-by-step guide to US insurance participation and billing activation.

Educational guide, not a payer-specific approval guarantee. Verify current requirements with each payer and state. Never enter patient or provider sensitive data here.

Credentialing, enrollment and contracting

Credentialing verifies qualifications, license, training, work history and professional standing. Enrollment registers a provider or organization with a payer for billing privileges. Contracting establishes network participation, rates and obligations. Privileging is a separate facility decision about permitted clinical services.

1. Collect provider information

Identify individual versus group, specialty, state, practice locations, legal name, ownership, TIN, Type 1 individual NPI and Type 2 organization NPI where applicable. Collect signed W-9, licenses, CV with explained gaps, education, board certification if applicable, malpractice certificate and history, DEA or state controlled substance registration if required, hospital privileges or coverage arrangements where required, and disclosures. Use secure storage; never publish SSN, DOB, banking or credentials.

2. NPI and taxonomy

Use NPPES to obtain or update NPI and taxonomy. Type 1 identifies an individual; Type 2 identifies an eligible organization. Match legal names and practice information to enrollment records. NPI alone does not establish payer participation.

3. CAQH Provider Data Portal

Create or update the profile where the payer uses CAQH. Complete all sections, upload current documents, authorize relevant payers to access the profile, and attest. Follow the portal's reattestation deadline (commonly 120 days, with exceptions). CAQH completion is not payer approval.

4. Medicare enrollment

Use PECOS or the applicable CMS paper application. CMS-855I is generally for individual physicians and nonphysician practitioners; CMS-855B for eligible clinics/group practices and certain suppliers; CMS-855A for institutional providers; CMS-855S for DMEPOS suppliers; CMS-855O for eligible ordering/certifying practitioners only. Reassignment is handled through PECOS or CMS-855I as applicable. CMS-588 handles EFT where required. Confirm current forms and MAC instructions; application type depends on provider category.

5. Medicaid enrollment

Apply through the relevant state Medicaid agency. Complete screening, ownership disclosures, license checks and any required fees or site visits. Managed care plans may require separate contracting and enrollment after state enrollment. For New York consult eMedNY and the relevant managed care plan.

6. Commercial payer enrollment

Identify exact payer, product, network, state and specialty. Check whether the panel is open. Submit participation request, CAQH authorization or payer application, roster and required documents. Track application reference, submission date, outstanding items and contact. A group contract does not automatically enroll every rendering provider or location.

7. Verification and committee review

Payers may perform primary source verification of licensure, education, sanctions, malpractice and other required qualifications. Respond to missing items securely. Check OIG LEIE and SAM exclusions as applicable and follow organizational screening policies. Do not represent pending applications as approved.

8. Contract and effective date

Review fee schedule, products, termination, timely filing, appeals and obligations before authorized signature. Obtain written confirmation of effective date, participating products, provider NPI, billing TIN and locations. Do not assume retroactive participation or bill as in-network before the confirmed date.

9. Billing activation and testing

Confirm payer roster and directory, rendering-to-billing affiliation, payer ID and clearinghouse EDI enrollment. Set up EFT and ERA separately when required. Validate claim configuration and monitor first adjudicated claims. Eligibility verification alone does not prove provider participation.

10. Maintenance and recredentialing

Track license, malpractice, CAQH attestation, payer recredentialing and Medicare revalidation deadlines. Report ownership, address, reassignment and other changes within applicable program deadlines. Recredentialing is commonly on a three-year cycle for many commercial plans but varies; Medicare revalidation is a separate process.

Follow-up and common problems

Track payer-specific expected turnaround rather than promising a fixed 30–90 day approval. Common issues: expired documents, CAQH access missing, NPI/TIN mismatch, closed panel, incomplete roster, wrong product, pending contract or missing effective date. Follow up using payer instructions and document reference numbers. For CO-185 or other provider-related denials, review actual CARC/RARC, payer enrollment and DOS effective date; appeal or correct only when supported.

Practical example: new physician joining a group

A new physician joins an existing group. Verify individual NPI, group NPI/TIN, license and CAQH. Submit Medicare reassignment and applicable enrollment changes, state Medicaid enrollment and each commercial payer's roster or application. Obtain written effective dates for each product and location, activate billing and verify first claims. Hold or route pending claims according to payer rules and office policy; do not miss filing deadlines.
